    Wet lab¶

    Access to all wet lab spaces requires a valid key and keycard to access spaces. Contact me during your first week to gain access to the spaces relevant to your project.

    Oregon Coastal Ocean Simulation Laboratory¶

    OCOSL is a flow-through seawater laboratory with continuous control of temperature, dissolved oxygen, and pH using automated aquaria control systems. A few designs include a triplicate manifold (three distinct treatments) fed to 150 small volume chambers or ~24 Hatfield Ultra-Dense Larval Systems (HULDS). The control system catered to oyster larvae rearing was dubbed as the Hatfield Aquatic Dynamic Environmental System (HADES) by MacKenna Hainey, and optimized in collaboration with Drs. Francis Chan and Kristopher Bauer.

    Hormetic Priming Laboratory¶

    Devoted space to the testing of dose-dependent effects across a spectrum of treatment conditions. Contains capacity for 48 replicate downweller tanks and continuous live algae feed.

    Quarantined Experimental Laboratory¶

    Pending early 2027. A parallel operating system to OCOSL is under construction in our flow-through quarantined space. All effluent in this space is treated, permitting experiments with non-native species and animals collected outside of state lines.

    Greenhouse and Workshop¶

    A large-footprint, flow-through seawater facility housing raceways and 10,000-gallon tanks, purpose-built for integrated multitrophic aquaculture (IMTA) research and coculture testing. The scale and design of this space allow the lab to work at densities and system complexities that are not achievable on the bench, making it well suited for evaluating the efficacy of emerging aquaculture methods and their readiness for industry adoption.

    Current and planned uses include IMTA system design, macroalgae production and coculture trials, integrated thermal acclimation (ITA) experiments, and quarantined effluent work. The space is intended as a testbed where novel approaches (species pairings, feeding strategies, environmental conditioning regimes) can be evaluated at meaningful scale before informing hatchery or farm-level practice.

    The workshop side of this space houses power tools, carpentry and plumbing supplies, and project materials for building and maintaining experimental systems.     Cold Storage¶

    Three temperature-controlled storage zones: a −20 °C freezer, a −80 °C ultra-low freezer, and a walk-in cold room at −8 °C.

    Courtyard¶

    An outdoor flow-through seawater facility housing troughs, raceways, and large industry-scale macroalgae tanks, all fed by ambient Yaquina Bay seawater and fitted with quarantine effluent treatment. The quarantined effluent system is a key feature: it permits the return and holding of animals collected from field deployments both in and out of state, as well as testing at industry-relevant scale without biosecurity risk to other HMSC systems.

    This space is particularly well suited for acclimation and recovery of field-collected animals, large-scale macroalgae culture and harvest trials, and experiments designed to evaluate aquaculture practices at the size and density relevant to commercial operations. All systems are fed flow-through ambient seawater, providing ecologically grounded water quality without the overhead of recirculating infrastructure.

    Organism husbandry¶

    All live animal work at HMSC is governed by the HMSC Animal Care Plan (ACP), overseen by the HMSC Animal Care Committee and advised by the facility's resident veterinarian, Dr. Tim Miller-Morgan. This applies to every system in the GurrLab: wet lab tanks, raceways, the Static Hatchery, the Courtyard, the Greenhouse, and any other space where live post-larval nonplanktonic invertebrates or vertebrates are held in captivity.

    The ACP is not optional and is not limited to formal experiments. Holding animals for acclimation, observation, or staging counts as captivity under the plan. If you are responsible for animals in any GurrLab space, the ACP governs how you care for them.

    ↗ Download the HMSC Animal Care Plan (PDF)

    What the ACP requires of every lab member¶

    Animal care protocols. All activities involving invertebrate animals must follow the Committee-approved Guidelines for Animal Care whenever possible. These are living documents. The most current version is on the HMSC ACP webpage. If your work cannot follow the Guidelines, you must document and follow best ethical practices and consult the Committee.

    Labeling. Every tank, container, or system holding invertebrates must display a lab tag with: (1) the responsible person's name, (2) their unit, (3) contact information, (4) species held, and (5) dates in captivity. For vertebrate work, the approved IACUC ACUP number must be displayed before the project begins.

    Monitoring and notification. You must notify the Committee chair by email before starting, ending, or significantly changing any activities involving animals. A Committee representative can conduct welfare checks at any time and must have access to your space and records. Welfare concerns will be communicated in writing and must be addressed promptly. Failure to do so can result in revocation of space use approval.

    Flow-through saltwater system. All animals using the open flow-through saltwater system must follow HMSC's requirements for that system, available on the ACP webpage.

    Unannounced welfare checks

    Dr. Tim Miller-Morgan and Animal Care Committee representatives conduct unannounced checks across all HMSC facilities. All systems must be maintained, labeled, and documented at all times, not just when an inspection is expected. If you notice a welfare concern in any lab space, report it to me immediately.

    Animal care support

    The Committee Chair can connect you with veterinary support, facility reviews, pre-diagnostic services, life support consultations, and equipment loan. If you are unsure whether your setup meets the Guidelines, ask before you start.
